Dinner here starts with twelve small plates.

Al Ateima — "the foods" — is a Middle-Eastern and Mediterranean kitchen built around the mezze table: hummus whipped to order, man'oushe flattened by hand, kofta off the charcoal, salads that taste like the lemon was cut a minute ago. Because it was.

The idea

One long table, passed left to right.

In every kitchen we come from — Beirut, Amman, the Aegean coast — dinner isn't a plate, it's a surface. Small dishes keep arriving until the table disappears, and everyone eats from everything. The meal ends when the bread does.

Al Ateima is built to serve that way: mezze first and always, charcoal mains for the center of the table, and a bread oven that doesn't stop. We're fitting out the room now, and the oven arrives before the chairs do. Priorities.

The pantry

Four things we refuse to buy pre-made.

Most of a Middle-Eastern menu is decided before the stove turns on — in the pantry. These four live or die on freshness, so we make them in-house, in small batches, on a schedule the kitchen plans around.

  • Za'atar blend Wild thyme, sumac, and sesame toasted the same day it's mixed. Jarred za'atar is a different, sadder spice.
  • Tahini Stone-ground sesame, stirred by hand. It's the backbone of half the cold mezze, so it gets no shortcuts.
  • Toum The garlic cloud. Emulsified fresh in the afternoon; by closing it's gone, and that's how it should be.
  • Pita & man'oushe dough Mixed each morning, proofed slow, baked all service. The oven is the first thing we bought.
